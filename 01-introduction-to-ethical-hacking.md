# 🎓 Introduction to Ethical Hacking

## 📌 Introduction
Ethical hacking is the authorized practice of probing systems, networks, and applications for
security weaknesses using the same tools and techniques a malicious attacker would use — but with
permission, scope, and the goal of fixing what's found rather than exploiting it.

---

## 🎯 Objectives
- Understand core information security concepts (CIA triad, non-repudiation, authenticity).
- Learn the classifications of hackers and types of penetration tests.
- Understand the five phases of hacking used throughout the CEH methodology.
- Understand relevant cyber laws and the importance of authorization.
- Understand risk management basics (residual risk, defense in depth).

---

## 🧑‍💻 Hacker Classifications
- **White Hat** – authorized security professional (ethical hacker).
- **Black Hat** – unauthorized, malicious intent.
- **Gray Hat** – unauthorized, but not necessarily malicious (may disclose responsibly).
- **Suicide Hacker** – acts without regard for consequences/getting caught.
- **Script Kiddie** – uses existing tools/scripts without deep technical understanding.
- **Hacktivist** – hacks to promote a political/social agenda.
- **State-Sponsored Hacker** – works on behalf of a nation-state (often linked to APTs).

---

## 🔐 Elements of Information Security
- **Confidentiality** – information is only accessible to authorized parties.
- **Integrity** – information is accurate and unaltered.
- **Availability** – information/systems are accessible when needed.
- **Authenticity** – confirms the genuineness of data/communication/user.
- **Non-Repudiation** – a party cannot deny an action they performed (e.g., via digital signatures).

---

## 🧭 The Five Phases of Hacking
1. **Reconnaissance** – gathering information about the target (passive/active).
2. **Scanning** – identifying live hosts, open ports, and services.
3. **Gaining Access** – exploiting a vulnerability to enter the system.
4. **Maintaining Access** – establishing persistence (backdoors, hidden accounts).
5. **Covering Tracks** – removing evidence of the intrusion.

---

## 🧪 Types of Penetration Testing
- **Black-Box** – no prior knowledge of the target (simulates an external attacker).
- **White-Box** – full knowledge (source code, architecture, credentials).
- **Gray-Box** – partial knowledge (simulates an insider or partially-informed attacker).
- **Double-Blind** – neither testers nor the defending team are informed in advance.
- **Announced vs. Unannounced** – whether the target's staff know testing is happening.

## 🛡️ Red Team vs. Blue Team vs. Purple Team
- **Red Team** – simulates real adversaries across people, process, and technology.
- **Blue Team** – defenders responsible for detection and response.
- **Purple Team** – collaborative exercise combining Red and Blue to improve detection.

---

## ⚖️ Cyber Laws & Standards Referenced
- **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)** – primary U.S. law on unauthorized computer access.
- **GDPR / HIPAA / PCI-DSS / SOX** – relevant data protection and compliance frameworks.
- **Rules of Engagement (RoE)** – the document that defines scope, boundaries, timing, and legal
  authorization for a penetration test — always obtain this before testing.

---

## 📊 Risk Management Basics
- **Threat** – a potential cause of an unwanted incident.
- **Vulnerability** – a weakness that can be exploited.
- **Risk** – the likelihood and impact of a threat exploiting a vulnerability.
- **Residual Risk** – risk remaining after controls are applied.
- **Defense in Depth** – layering multiple independent controls so no single failure compromises
  the whole system.

---

## 🧰 Common Terms
- **Zero-Day** – a vulnerability unknown to the vendor with no available patch.
- **APT (Advanced Persistent Threat)** – prolonged, targeted, well-resourced intrusion.
- **Attack Surface** – the sum of all points where an attacker could try to enter or extract data.

---

## 📄 Reporting
A penetration test engagement typically starts and ends with documentation:
- **Scope document / RoE** – defines what's allowed before testing starts.
- **Final report** – findings, severity, evidence, and remediation recommendations.

---

## ⚠️ Disclaimer
This content is for **educational and authorized penetration testing only**. Unauthorized access
to computer systems is illegal under laws such as the CFAA and equivalent legislation worldwide.
